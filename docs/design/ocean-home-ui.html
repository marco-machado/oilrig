Foundations

The Ocean Home design system

Three ideas from the platform itself, applied to every surface.

Rig yellow is the only accent

The color of the steel. It marks the one action that matters on each screen, what is switched on, and keyboard focus. Nothing else is yellow.

Surfaces are frosted glass

Like the pavilion's walls: deep-sea tinted, blurred, with a thin bright top edge. The world always shows through.

Names in serif, numbers in mono

The name and screen titles in a quiet serif; times, speeds and frame times in a monospaced face, like instruments on a bridge.

Motion

Quick in, quicker out

Things enter with a soft deceleration and leave faster than they came. Movement is short (8 to 20 px) and never blocks input. With reduced motion turned on, everything appears at once.

What changes

Your six points, in the code

Nothing below is built yet. This is the plan once the design is approved.

1

One design system

Loading, sign in, menu, settings, time & weather, performance: one direction.

  • New ui/tokens.css with the color, type, space, radius, glass and motion tokens on this page.
  • The loading screen in index.html reads the same tokens instead of its own inline colors.
  • Fonts self-hosted from npm (@fontsource), so the game makes no font requests to Google.
2

Proper components

Create proper UI components.

  • ui/components/: button, icon button, field, switch, segmented, slider, sky dial, row group, sheet, dialog, pill, keycap, stat grid, frame graph.
  • ui.ts composes screens from them; style.css splits into tokens, components and screens.
3

Log out

Add a logout button to the menu.

  • Log out at the foot of the menu rail, with a confirmation dialog.
  • On confirm: fade out, net.disconnect(), clear avatars, players and chat, reset the drone seat and the joined state, show sign in with the name kept.
  • The server already treats a closed socket as leaving: it frees the drone seat and tells everyone (world.disconnect).
4

Tasteful motion

Menu, screens, modals.

  • The patterns in the Motion table, as CSS keyframes driven by the motion tokens.
  • Exits play before an element is hidden, so nothing pops away.
  • prefers-reduced-motion turns all of it off.
5

A menu that belongs somewhere

The menu looks wrongly placed.

  • The floating toolbar goes. Esc opens a full-height rail on the left; one section opens beside it at a time.
  • Performance becomes its own readout in the top-right corner (F3), separate from the menu.
  • The status chips become one capsule; presence moves into a matching pill.
6

Settings, time and performance, redesigned

They look designed by a junior designer.

  • Settings: grouped rows with plain descriptions, a segmented quality control, switches, values shown in mono.
  • Time & weather: a large clock, the sky dial, a segmented speed with a caption, and weather as tiles with Forecast set apart.
  • Performance: FPS and a health label first, a labelled frame-time graph, a stat grid, CPU by system as bars.

Decisions for you

  • Fonts

    Three self-hosted families add roughly 150–200 KB of woff2 to the first load. The alternative is system fonts, which loses the serif and the instrument mono.

  • Controls gets its own section

    The key list leaves Settings and the sign-in card and becomes Controls in the menu, with the drone keys added. Sign in keeps a single line of essentials.

  • “Come aboard” replaces “Enter”

    On the sign-in button. Offline it still reads “Explore offline”.

  • Quality still reloads at once

    Same behavior as today; the row now says it will reload before you pick.

  • Time-of-day label

    The words under the clock (Morning, Golden hour, Night) would come from the sun's elevation in astronomy.ts; the prototype uses fixed hours.
